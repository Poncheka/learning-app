# PLINY
## Brand Identity & Creative Direction
**Version 1.1 — Experimental Editorial**

### 01. Brand overview

**Brand:** Pliny  
**Category:** Personalized audio learning  
**Creative direction:** Experimental Editorial

**Brand idea: Get lost in something.**

Pliny makes learning about almost anything feel spontaneous, fascinating, and entertaining. It transforms curiosity into personalized audio experiences, giving people a new way to explore the world around them.

The brand should communicate intellectual curiosity without feeling academic, and playfulness without feeling childish.

**Brand personality**

- **Curious:** Fascinated by the strange, overlooked, and unexpected
- **Playful:** Clever, occasionally irreverent, never childish
- **Confident:** Bold in its visual expression, without taking itself too seriously
- **Intelligent:** Thoughtful and sophisticated, never pretentious
- **Unexpected:** Willing to be strange, surprising, and unconventional
- **Approachable:** Welcoming to anyone with a question

**Creative philosophy: Serious design. Unserious curiosity.**

Pliny should feel like a contemporary independent magazine made for people who find the world endlessly fascinating.

---

### 02. Creative direction

**Experimental Editorial**

Pliny's visual identity draws from contemporary editorial design, experimental typography, independent publishing, and modern graphic art.

It combines strong typographic hierarchy, bold color, geometric compositions, and unexpected visual relationships.

The result should feel culturally relevant, contemporary, intellectually stimulating, and distinctly original.

**Visual characteristics**

- Oversized editorial typography
- High-contrast compositions
- Expressive geometric shapes
- Unconventional cropping and scale
- Purposeful asymmetry
- Generous negative space
- Selective use of photography and halftone treatments
- Sophisticated use of vivid colors
- Minimal decorative clutter
- Unexpected combinations of visual elements

The brand should balance boldness and restraint.

**Design principle: One surprising visual idea is more powerful than ten decorative elements.**

#### Creative references

**Independent editorial publishing**

Large typography, unconventional layouts, sophisticated image treatments, and carefully considered negative space.

**Swiss modernist graphic design**

Strong grids, visual hierarchy, geometric simplicity, and clarity.

**Experimental poster design**

Unexpected proportions, expressive composition, and creative tension between typography and imagery.

**Contemporary cultural branding**

Bold personality, visual versatility, and a recognizable point of view.

**Teenage Engineering**

An example of how technical sophistication and playful visual expression can coexist.

These are directional references, not templates to reproduce.

The palette should feel energetic and contemporary without becoming chaotic.

#### Foundational colors

| Color | Hex | Character |
|---|---|---|

- Neutrals provide clarity and visual consistency.
- Expressive colors provide energy and personality.

- Excessive pastel palettes
- Overly muted, earthy color systems
- Rainbow-colored compositions without hierarchy
- Glowing neon effects
- Decorative color without a clear purpose

The brand should feel bold and graphic, not fluorescent or overwhelming.

### 04. Typography

Typography is the defining element of Pliny's identity.

The system should balance editorial personality, contemporary expression, and clarity.

#### Primary editorial typeface: Instrument Serif

**Styles:** Regular and Italic

Instrument Serif brings sophistication, curiosity, and a slightly unconventional literary quality.

It should be used for expressive editorial statements, brand messaging, and moments where personality matters more than strict utility.

Its italic form is particularly valuable for introducing visual contrast.

Examples:

*What are you curious about?*

*Get lost in something.*

#### Expressive display typeface: Space Grotesk

**Weights:** Medium, Bold

Space Grotesk provides a contemporary counterpoint to the editorial serif.

It is well suited to oversized headlines, graphic compositions, and visually assertive statements.

It can be set in uppercase, with tight spacing and dramatic line breaks.

For especially compressed typographic treatments, **Archivo Narrow Black** may be explored as a supplementary option.

Examples:

**THE GREAT  
EMU WAR.**

**A WORLD  
OF WHAT IF.**

#### Supporting sans-serif: Inter

**Weights:** Regular, Medium, Semibold

Inter serves as the neutral typographic counterpart to Pliny's expressive display typography.

It is appropriate for secondary copy, explanatory text, labels, and functional brand applications.

#### Typographic principles

- Use typography as imagery, not merely as a vehicle for information.
- Embrace dramatic differences in scale.
- Explore unconventional line breaks.
- Use bold and italic contrasts purposefully.
- Favor strong alignment and clear hierarchy.
- Allow large letterforms to interact with geometric shapes.
- Use uppercase for emphasis and graphic impact.
- Maintain legibility even in experimental compositions.
- Avoid excessive font mixing.
- Preserve a consistent visual relationship between expressive and functional typography.

**Avoid**

- Generic futuristic fonts
- Decorative handwritten typography
- Childlike lettering
- Excessive font effects
- Unnecessary outlines, shadows, or distortions
- Dense typography without a clear hierarchy

The goal is typography that feels expressive, intelligent, and culturally contemporary.

### 05. Graphic language

Pliny's graphic language should feel experimental, geometric, and conceptually connected to the subjects it represents.

Graphics should create a sense of discovery and visual surprise.

#### Core visual elements

**Geometry**
- Circles and semicircles
- Arcs and concentric forms
- Angular planes
- Cropped geometric shapes
- Thin lines and structural grids
- Repetition and pattern

**Editorial treatments**
- Halftone photography
- High-contrast monochrome imagery
- Asymmetric cropping
- Layered geometric forms
- Oversized typographic elements
- Unexpected scale relationships

**Accent elements**
- Small starbursts
- Graphic punctuation
- Simple arrows
- Dot patterns
- Minimal line details

Accent elements should have a recognizable stylistic consistency.

#### Composition principles

- Establish one clear visual focal point.
- Use contrast in size, weight, and placement.
- Balance expressive elements with substantial negative space.
- Allow typography and imagery to interact.
- Favor conceptual visual metaphors over literal illustration.
- Use asymmetry with intentional structure.
- Introduce humor through unexpected juxtaposition.
- Avoid filling available space simply because it exists.

**A composition should feel deliberately designed rather than algorithmically decorated.**

### 06. Illustration and photography

Pliny should avoid a traditional illustrated educational aesthetic.

Imagery should feel more like contemporary editorial art, experimental photography, and modern poster design.

#### Preferred imagery

- Cropped photographic subjects
- Halftone and duotone photography
- Abstract visual metaphors
- Geometric interpretations of real objects
- Unexpected object isolation
- Architectural fragments
- Scientific or historical imagery treated graphically
- Minimal, conceptually driven compositions

#### Artistic character

Imagery should be:
- Bold
- Slightly strange
- Thoughtful
- Visually surprising
- Recognizable
- Sophisticated
- Occasionally humorous

#### Avoid

- Cartoon characters
- Children's book illustration
- Generic stock photography
- Conventional podcast cover art
- AI-generated fantasy imagery
- Glossy 3D objects
- Overly realistic CGI
- Excessive gradients
- Generic flat-vector illustrations
- Busy illustrative scenes

A single unusual object, beautifully cropped and placed against a bold geometric form, may be more compelling than an elaborate illustration.

### 07. Lesson artwork identity

Each Pliny lesson can have a distinctive editorial artwork treatment.

The objective is to make every topic feel like a new discovery while maintaining a cohesive brand identity.

Think of individual lessons as covers of an endlessly expanding independent magazine.

#### Artwork principles

**Typography-forward**

Large, confident typography is a central part of the visual language.

**Conceptually relevant**

Visual elements should connect to the subject through imagery, metaphor, or unexpected association.

**Visually distinctive**

Each subject should inspire its own composition rather than being inserted into a rigid template.

**Recognizably Pliny**

Even with different colors and subjects, artwork should share consistent typography, compositional principles, and graphic treatments.

**Flexible**

The art direction should translate across square, portrait, and landscape formats.

#### Artwork composition families

**A. Typographic**

Bold display lettering dominates the composition, supported by minimal graphic elements.

**B. Photographic**

One carefully selected subject is combined with geometric forms or expressive typography.

**C. Abstract**

Geometric forms, patterns, and visual metaphors represent an idea or concept.

**D. Diagrammatic**

Scientific, historical, or conceptual material is reinterpreted through a sophisticated editorial graphic language.

**E. Collage**

Photography, typography, and geometry are combined into an intentionally layered composition.

These families provide variety without abandoning a recognizable visual identity.

#### Artwork rules

- Prioritize one strong central idea.
- Limit the expressive color palette to approximately two or three colors.
- Favor high contrast.
- Avoid unnecessary visual complexity.
- Maintain recognizable subjects when photography is used.
- Keep title treatments legible.
- Use negative space deliberately.
- Avoid repetitive compositions across unrelated subjects.

### 08. AI-generated artwork standards

Generative artwork should be treated as a component of a defined creative system, not a source of random visual styles.

The objective is consistency of art direction while preserving subject-specific creativity.

#### Master generation prompt

Create an experimental editorial artwork for Pliny, a contemporary audio-learning brand.

**Subject:** [TOPIC]

**Visual concept:** [CONCEPT OR METAPHOR]

**Art direction:**

Contemporary independent magazine design, experimental editorial graphics, Swiss modernist influences, bold geometric composition, and sophisticated visual storytelling.

Use strong contrast, intentional negative space, purposeful asymmetry, and unexpected relationships between visual elements.

Incorporate subject-relevant imagery through selective photography, halftone treatments, abstract geometry, or conceptual collage.

**Color:**

Use a restrained palette consisting of one dominant expressive color, one or two supporting colors, and neutral charcoal or off-white.

**Composition:**

Create one clear focal point. Favor striking simplicity over complexity. Use dramatic cropping, scale contrast, and graphic tension.

The result should feel intelligent, playful, contemporary, and visually surprising.

**Avoid:**

Cartoon illustration, children's book aesthetics, stock photography, conventional podcast covers, generic AI-generated imagery, fantasy art, glossy 3D, excessive gradients, and overly decorative compositions.

**Output:**

Create a clean, high-resolution editorial composition without text, lettering, or logos. Typography will be added separately using the brand's typography system.

#### Recommended production approach

**1. Concept development**

Identify a distinctive visual idea or metaphor connected to the subject.

**2. Image generation**

Create artwork following Pliny's established visual vocabulary.

**3. Editorial composition**

Add brand typography, geometric accents, and final layout using a consistent design system.

**4. Quality review**

Verify visual clarity, conceptual relevance, and consistency with the brand.

Separating image generation from typography is recommended because it provides greater consistency and control over the final artwork.

#### Creative variability

The brand should encourage visual experimentation within its established rules.

A lesson about ancient Rome does not need to share the same visual composition as a lesson about black holes.

Consistency should come from art direction, not identical templates.

### 09. Motion identity

Motion can extend Pliny's visual personality beyond static imagery.

It should feel intentional, sophisticated, and subtly experimental.

#### Motion characteristics

- Smooth
- Precise
- Rhythmic
- Geometric
- Occasionally unexpected
- Never unnecessarily distracting

#### Potential visual expressions

- Shapes expanding and contracting
- Typography appearing through elegant transitions
- Geometric forms assembling and separating
- Abstract lines responding to sound
- Subtle shifts in scale and composition
- Repeated patterns evolving over time

Motion should reinforce the brand's sense of curiosity and exploration.

Avoid exaggerated bouncing, excessive particle effects, generic loading animations, and movement that feels decorative rather than intentional.

### 10. Verbal identity

Pliny's written personality should be curious, witty, and confidently conversational.

The brand should sound like someone who knows a surprising amount about the world but never makes you feel stupid for asking.

#### Tone attributes

**Curious, not academic**

Encourage exploration rather than emphasizing formal education.

**Smart, not pretentious**

Make unusual or complex subjects approachable.

**Playful, not goofy**

Use humor selectively and intelligently.

**Confident, not corporate**

Prefer direct, distinctive language over generic marketing terminology.

**Surprising, not sensational**

Create intrigue without relying on exaggerated clickbait.

#### Example brand language

**Get lost in something.**

**A little knowledge goes a long way.**

**For the endlessly curious.**

**Something you didn't know you wanted to know.**

**The world is weirder than you think.**

**Curiosity looks good on you.**

#### Writing guidelines

- Favor short, memorable phrases.
- Lead with intrigue.
- Use conversational language.
- Allow occasional dry humor.
- Avoid exaggerated promises.
- Avoid overly technical descriptions.
- Never talk down to the audience.

### 11. Brand guardrails

Pliny should never feel like:

**A traditional podcast brand**

Avoid familiar podcast cover conventions, microphones, headphones, sound-wave logos, or streaming-service visual tropes.

**A children's education brand**

Avoid cartoon mascots, playful classroom imagery, or excessively rounded, juvenile graphic treatments.

**A generic AI startup**

Avoid purple-blue gradients, glowing orbs, futuristic iconography, and visual clichés about artificial intelligence.

**A wellness or meditation brand**

Avoid overly soft, atmospheric, subdued, or tranquil design language.

**An academic institution**

Avoid institutional formality, historical pastiche, and overly traditional educational imagery.

**A chaotic experimental art project**

Boldness should not compromise clarity, consistency, or recognizability.

The brand should occupy the space between intellectual sophistication and joyful experimentation.

### 12. Brand evaluation criteria

Creative work should be evaluated against the following questions:

1. Does this feel contemporary and culturally relevant?
2. Does it communicate intelligence without pretension?
3. Is there a sense of curiosity or surprise?
4. Does the typography feel distinctive?
5. Is color being used confidently and purposefully?
6. Does the composition balance experimentation with restraint?
7. Could this be confused with a traditional podcast or education brand?
8. Does the artwork feel thoughtfully designed rather than randomly generated?
9. Is it recognizable as part of a cohesive visual identity?
10. Does it feel like Pliny?
